Lead story
US commits $99M to accelerate an Ebola vaccine as DRC's outbreak holds above 10,300 cases

CDC's Oct 30 update puts the Bundibugyo-virus outbreak at 10,305 total confirmed cases and 4,996 deaths (10,274 cases / 4,992 deaths inside DRC) across eight provinces, plus nine cases and two deaths in Kenya and one case in Nigeria — the second-largest Ebola outbreak on record. On Oct 29, ASPR/BARDA committed $99M (building on $36M already confirmed) to IAVI, Hilleman Laboratories, SK Bioscience and IDT Biologika to speed manufacturing scale-up and a ring-vaccination trial for a Bundibugyo-strain shot built on the same rVSV platform as the WHO-prequalified Zaire-strain vaccine Ervebo. The US travel-attestation requirement for travelers from the affected region took effect Nov 3.

Why it matters

  • At roughly a 48% case-fatality ratio, the human toll is rising in step with the case count, not slower — a sign containment is still not ahead of transmission.
  • No licensed vaccine yet exists for Bundibugyo ebolavirus; the US funding is a bet on compressing the manufacturing timeline before the outbreak outruns it.
  • A new US entry rule signals international concern is hardening into policy, with knock-on implications for travel, trade and regional diplomacy.

What to watch

  • TUMAINI trial enrollment numbers and any early efficacy signal from the phase 2/3 study that began enrolling in Bunia on Oct 23.
  • Whether cross-border cases spread beyond Kenya and Nigeria.
  • Compliance and friction effects from the Nov 3 US travel-attestation rule.

Biocatalysis Pulse Analysis

2 articles · Oct 21–29

Biocatalysis news stayed quiet through the Nov 1–3 window, with no new company or deal news confirmed for either Codexis or Novonesis — both now have earnings dates imminent (Novonesis's 9M 2026 interim report lands Nov 5, Codexis's Q3 print Nov 12), which should finally test whether ECO Synthesis's recent oligonucleotide partnerships and Patalganga capacity investment are converting to revenue. In the literature, the design-plus-evolution playbook keeps compounding: this week's PubMed feed adds a trimethyllysine-binding chromodomain built by directed evolution, a site-attention kinetics-prediction model (SAEKP), and a biocatalytic redox cascade for direct β-alkylation of cyclopentanones, alongside continued work on protein-catenane topology and Rubisco evolution — evidence that academic throughput on enzyme engineering is outpacing the deal flow needed to commercialize it.

Three actionable insights

  1. Two earnings prints in eight days will reset the sector's read. Novonesis (Nov 5) and Codexis (Nov 12) both report before this edition's next full cycle — watch for whether ECO Synthesis and precision-fermentation enzyme demand actually shows up in guidance.
  2. Design is commoditizing; value is moving to data and scale-up. With leading enzyme-design methods now published and partly open-sourced, weight proprietary screening data, reaction scope and process know-how over the design claim itself.
  3. AI-guided kinetics and evolution tools are multiplying faster than deal flow. This week's literature (site-attention kinetics prediction, directed-evolution binder design) suggests a widening gap between research-tool availability and the commercial biocatalysis deals needed to monetize them — a gap worth watching for consolidation or licensing activity.

    Agriculture Pulse Analysis

    12 articles · 10 global, 2 Brazil

    Brazil's 2026/27 soybean campaign kept outrunning last year's pace: Conab put national planting at 42.8% of area as of Oct 23 (vs. 34.8% a year earlier and a 40.2% five-year average, with Mato Grosso already at 75.8%), even as Secex data show only 4.818Mt exported in the first three weeks of October versus 6.728Mt for all of October 2025 — though the average export price rose 14.4% y/y to $489.3/t, a sign buyers are paying up even as early-month volume lags. Input-cost pressure kept building in parallel: DTN's retailer survey showed US fertilizer prices climbing for a third straight week (UAN32 up 6% to $486/ton, anhydrous ammonia up 5% to $987/ton and 18% y/y), which DTN ties to the Strait of Hormuz conflict, Chinese export curbs, a global sulfur shortage and Nutrien's indefinite Trinidad plant shutdown — a supply shock landing just as USDA finalizes $1.34B across eight domestic fertilizer-expansion projects. Meanwhile FMC's Q3 print cratered to roughly $106M in GAAP revenue (from ~$542M a year ago), and a separate analysis found biologicals R&D spending at the five largest crop-protection majors up 43% since 2019 while chemical R&D fell 9%. Smaller, specialized funding kept flowing — Terradot's $34M carbon-removal loan, BloomX's $13M robotic-pollination round, Volare's €7.1M insect-protein plant and IMIO's biostimulant seed round.

    Three actionable insights

    1. Brazil is exporting less volume at a higher price, not more volume. October's early-month soybean shipments trail last year by nearly 2Mt even as the average price is up 14.4% y/y — watch whether that's buyers paying up for scarcity or merely a timing shift into November.
    2. A fertilizer supply shock is colliding with a US capacity build-out. Three straight weeks of US retail fertilizer price gains (Hormuz, Chinese export curbs, a sulfur shortage, Nutrien's Trinidad shutdown) land just as USDA's $1.34B expansion program breaks ground — the near-term price pain and the multi-year capacity fix are on very different clocks.
    3. Biologicals R&D is outgrowing chemical R&D at the majors. A 43% rise in biologicals spending against a 9% chemical-R&D decline at the top five crop-protection companies since 2019 is a structural signal, not a one-quarter blip — worth tracking against FMC's own capital-allocation response to its Q3 reset.

        Synthetic Biology Pulse Analysis

        4 articles · Oct 28–Nov 3

        Precision fermentation carried this window's news. Formo and Those Vegan Cowboys landed what both companies describe as the first FDA "no questions" GRAS letter for precision-fermented animal-free casein, clearing it for use in cheese alternatives up to 25% inclusion — a regulatory first that matters more than its size, since it opens a path other precision-fermented dairy proteins can now follow. TurtleTree paired its precision-fermented lactoferrin with a research partnership with Dutch CRO Nizo Food Research to build clinical evidence for a women's-health (menopause) indication, while Cremer's FermBase platform expanded its AI/automation collaboration with Munich's Differential Bio to speed fermentation process development from early R&D through scale-up. Funding stayed small and specialized: Dutch foodtech Greencovery raised €1M to scale a platform that upcycles cocoa-industry sidestreams into bio-based fibres and proteins, seeking a further €3M to reach 1,000-tonne capacity.

        Three actionable insights

        1. A GRAS "first" for precision-fermented casein opens a template. Formo/Those Vegan Cowboys' FDA letter is a regulatory precedent other precision-fermented dairy-protein filings can now cite, likely compressing their own review timelines.
        2. Clinical validation is moving into precision-fermented ingredients, not just novel foods. TurtleTree's Nizo partnership targeting a menopause indication for fermented lactoferrin signals the category is reaching for pharma-style evidence standards, not just food-safety clearance.
        3. AI-driven process development is becoming a service layer on top of fermentation, not just a design tool. Cremer/Differential Bio's expanded collaboration suggests platform companies are outsourcing process optimization rather than building it in-house.

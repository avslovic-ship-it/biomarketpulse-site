Biocatalysis Pulse Analysis

3 articles · Oct 14–29

Biocatalysis news stayed thin over the long weekend, but the signal is consistent: enzymatic manufacturing keeps gaining ground in pharma supply chains, and the bottleneck is shifting from chemistry to engineering throughput. Codexis set its Q3 2026 earnings date (Nov 12) against reiterated $72–76M revenue guidance, a near-term test of whether its ECO Synthesis platform's recent oligonucleotide partnerships are converting to revenue. Fundamental research keeps expanding what enzymes can do outside biology's native repertoire: the Baker and Lewis labs' Nature paper on photocatalyst-binding protein scaffolds (carried from last week) remains the clearest demonstration that generative design plus directed evolution can deliver genuinely new-to-nature chemistry. Separately, Cemvita's tie-up with AI-native process-engineering firm Roebling points to a new category of bioprocess-to-plant accelerants aimed at shortening biocatalytic and fermentation scale-up timelines.

Three actionable insights

  1. Watch Codexis's Nov 12 print for ECO Synthesis commercial proof points. Recent siRNA and oligonucleotide partnership announcements need to show up in revenue, not just deal-flow headlines, to validate the platform thesis.
  2. Design is commoditizing; value is moving to data and scale-up. With leading enzyme-design methods now published and partly open-sourced, weight proprietary screening data, reaction scope and process know-how over the design claim itself.
  3. Bioprocess engineering-as-a-service could shorten the industrial-biotech scale-up gap. The Cemvita/Roebling partnership is an early example of AI-native process engineering firms attaching to biosolutions platforms — watch for similar tie-ups at other platform companies in the coming months.

Baker and Lewis labs design new-to-nature enzymes around man-made photocatalyst cofactors
Generatively designed protein scaffolds bind one mirror-image form of a metal polypyridine photocatalyst; directed evolution then turns them into efficient, highly enantioselective photocatalysts that recycle and accept cheap racemic cofactor. The clearest demonstration yet that design plus evolution can deliver chemistry no natural enzyme performs.

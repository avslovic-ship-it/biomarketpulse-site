Soybean rust: chemical against biological

All 142 registered rust products are chemical; 71% contain a triazole and 61% a strobilurin. A biological with a new mode of action would be the first on that shelf.

White mold has already gone biological

92 of 109 white-mold products are biological, almost all Trichoderma and Bacillus. Growers and distributors already buy biological fungicides here; a peptide competes on consistency, not adoption.

Peptide products registered in Brazil

Two harpin-derived peptide fungicides and one nematicide, all from the former Plant Health Care, now PI Industries. Two more peptide plant activators come from Elemental Enzymes. The peptide fungicide field is close to empty.

How the scores are built

Need = 40% single-site dependence (share of chemical fungicides whose every active sits in a medium- or high-risk FRAC group) + 35% shelf age (share of products whose actives were all on the Brazilian register before 2010) + 25% biological gap (full weight with no biological fungicide, less for each one held).

Reach = 60% size of the registered fungicide shelf + 40% soybean-rust coverage, both against the largest holder.

Capacity reads the latest quarter: sales trend, profit, guidance, balance-sheet stress and strategic moves such as spin-offs and listings.

Built from 4,252 Brazilian registrations, 10 latest earnings reports, 26 dated financial signals from results releases and news between Oct 1 and Nov 17, 2026 (plus earlier deal news), and 8 share-price series. Original articles and filings are held in the evidence trail, not shown here.

Limits. The registry counts registrations, not sales. Private companies have no public financials. Mode-of-action mapping is a draft awaiting agronomic review. This is market intelligence, not investment advice.
